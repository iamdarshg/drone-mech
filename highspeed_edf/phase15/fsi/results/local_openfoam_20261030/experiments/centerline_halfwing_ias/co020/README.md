# Reduced-Courant sensitivity on the experimental centerline mesh

This bounded sensitivity test uses the generated 49,208-cell experimental centerline half-wing mesh, fresh t=0 fields, transonic PIMPLE, PBiCGStab/DILU pressure, upwind transport, limited corrected laplacian, and three non-orthogonal corrections. Relative to the paired centerline experiment, the intended changes are `maxCo 0.2`, `deltaT 5e-6`, and `maxDeltaT 1e-5`. Mesh and geometry hashes are recorded in each case manifest.

Both speeds ran in two bounded segments: a fresh-from-zero segment capped at 300 s, followed by continuation from the latest complete checkpoint with a 600 s cap. Each continuation ended on its wall-clock cap with exit code 124; neither log contains a normal `End` marker. The final printed `Time =` is the start of an incomplete solver iteration, so it is not a completed timestep. The latest persisted checkpoints are IAS700 at 1.5442106e-4 s and IAS900 at 1.2588395e-4 s. The last logged attempted times were 1.5721079e-4 s and 1.3398241e-4 s, respectively. Exact logs, exit receipts, controls, and checkpoint histories are retained in this directory.

Checkpoint analysis was refreshed after the continuation. IAS700 at its latest persisted state reached T=179.1–1151.7 K and speed extrema are in `cases/checkpoint_history_ias700.json`; IAS900 reached T=173.8–728.4 K. These extreme thermal states indicate a developing nonphysical solution despite the reduced time-step cap. The runs tested sensitivity to maxCo only; neither converged and neither supports steady-load or design claims. Pressure loads are transient, omit viscous stress, and include the artificial inboard cut. The mesh lacks fuselage, root fillet, and EDF flow.

## Checkpoint trend review

The serialized checkpoint histories were reviewed after both capped continuations. IAS700 is the clearest instability signal: between its first and last persisted checkpoints (19.0 to 154.4 microseconds), maximum cell speed rose from 270.9 to 2114.0 m/s, maximum temperature from 290.1 to 1151.7 K, and pressure range widened from 53.0–103.4 kPa to 22.2–258.8 kPa. IAS900 shows the same direction over 15.2 to 125.9 microseconds: maximum speed 329.5 to 1038.0 m/s, maximum temperature 290.3 to 728.4 K, and pressure range 53.3–108.1 kPa to 36.2–193.5 kPa. These extrema may occur in different cells, so they are indicators of nonphysical evolution, not local state reconstructions.

The IAS700 pressure-only Z force changes from -79.7 N to -122.7 N across the persisted sequence; IAS900 changes from -88.2 N to -187.3 N. Those values are not usable design loads: the flow is diverging, the record is transient, viscous traction is omitted, and the artificial inboard cut remains in the pressure integral. The detailed per-checkpoint extrema, locations, boundary fluxes, and pressure forces are in `cases/checkpoint_history_ias700.json` and `cases/checkpoint_history_ias900.json`.

This diagnoses the current solver setup as unfit for load extraction even at maxCo=0.2. Extending these same runs is not productive: the late-state field excursions grow sharply as the solver approaches its wall cap, and neither run completes a normal solver termination. A defensible next solver investigation should return to a short fresh run and localize the first growth in pressure/temperature/speed against the mesh and boundary fields, then alter one numerical or setup factor at a time. Do not derive a load coefficient, infer convergence, or promote this mesh/case from these checkpoints.
